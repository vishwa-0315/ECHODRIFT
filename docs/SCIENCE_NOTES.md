# ECHO DRIFT: Science Notes

Every fact the game states, with its source and what was simplified.

## Gravity fades with distance

Twice as far from a planet, its gravity pulls only a quarter as hard. That's the inverse-square law Isaac Newton described.

- **Simplified:** The game uses Newton's law with scaled-down masses and distances, and a tiny 'softening' term so gravity never becomes infinite at a point.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Gravity](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** gravity-distance

## Orbits are falling

Astronauts float because they and their station are falling around Earth together, moving sideways so fast that they keep missing the ground.

- **Simplified:** Game orbits are flat (2D). Real orbits are 3D and slightly disturbed by other bodies.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Basics of Space Flight, Orbital Mechanics](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** orbits-falling

## Free speed from planets

Voyager 2 used flybys of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune to gain speed and change direction. It's now in interstellar space.

- **Simplified:** In the game the moon's speed is shown exaggerated so the effect is easy to feel.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Voyager](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** gravity-assist

## The iridium clue

In 1980, scientists found a thin clay layer rich in iridium all over Earth. It helped show that an asteroid impact about 66 million years ago played a key part in the dinosaurs' extinction.

- **Simplified:** Volcanic eruptions in India (the Deccan Traps) may also have played a role; scientists still discuss the details.
- **Source:** [Britannica: K–T extinction](https://www.britannica.com/science/K-T-extinction) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** superposition

## Deeper is older

In undisturbed rock, each layer is younger than the one below it. Geologists call this the law of superposition.

- **Simplified:** Folding and faulting can flip real rock layers; the game's layers are undisturbed.
- **Source:** [Britannica: Law of superposition](https://www.britannica.com/science/law-of-superposition) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** superposition

## Venus, the oven planet

Venus's air is about 96% carbon dioxide. Its surface is hotter than Mercury's, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.

- **Simplified:** Veil is a made-up world inspired by Venus.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Venus](https://science.nasa.gov/venus/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** greenhouse

## Stars brighten as they age

Sun-like stars slowly brighten. Our young Sun shone about 30% dimmer than it does today.

- **Simplified:** The FORGE time-lapse uses a simple 'about 10% brighter per billion years' rule.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: The Sun](https://science.nasa.gov/sun/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** star-change, habitable-zone

## The Goldilocks zone

The habitable zone is the range of distances from a star where a planet could have liquid water on its surface. Not too hot, not too cold.

- **Simplified:** Zone edges use a simple formula based on the star's brightness; real zones also depend on each planet's air.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Exoplanets](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** habitable-zone

## Braking with air

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter dipped into Mars's upper atmosphere hundreds of times in 2006 to slow down and shrink its orbit without using much fuel.

- **Simplified:** The game squeezes many careful passes into one dramatic skim.
- **Source:** [NASA JPL: Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-reconnaissance-orbiter/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** atmosphere-drag

## The two-burn trick

The Hohmann transfer, described by Walter Hohmann in 1925, moves a spacecraft between two circular orbits with two engine burns, using very little fuel.

- **Simplified:** Game burns happen instantly; real engines burn for minutes.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Basics of Space Flight, Interplanetary Trajectories](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** hohmann

## Calm spots in space

Lagrange points are places where a small object can keep pace with a planet. The James Webb Space Telescope orbits near one of them (Sun–Earth L2), and Jupiter has thousands of 'Trojan' asteroids at its L4 and L5 points.

- **Simplified:** The game shows L4, which is stable when the planet is much lighter than its star.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: What is a Lagrange Point?](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/what-is-a-lagrange-point/)
- **Concepts:** lagrange

## Black holes

Inside a black hole's event horizon, gravity is so strong that not even light can escape. We spot them by the X-rays from hot gas swirling in, and by how they tug on nearby stars.

- **Simplified:** The game uses ordinary Newtonian gravity right up to the horizon; real black holes need Einstein's general relativity.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Black Holes](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** black-holes

## Escape velocity

To leave Earth for good without more pushing, you'd need about 11.2 km/s. That's over 30 times the speed of sound!

- **Simplified:** Game speeds are scaled down to fit the screen.
- **Source:** [Britannica: Escape velocity](https://www.britannica.com/science/escape-velocity) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** escape-velocity

## Starlight is old news

Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to reach us, and from the nearest star, Proxima Centauri, about 4.2 years. We see stars as they were when the light left.

- **Simplified:** The Orrin system's 38 light-year distance is invented.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Light-year](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** light-delay

## Pulsars

Pulsars are spinning neutron stars that sweep beams across space like lighthouses. Jocelyn Bell Burnell discovered the first one in 1967. The Crab pulsar spins about 30 times a second.

- **Simplified:** The game shows pulse timing only, not the radio telescope data astronomers use.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Neutron Stars](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** light-delay

## Planet hunting with shadows

When a planet passes in front of its star, the starlight dips a tiny bit. Most known exoplanets were found this way, by missions like Kepler and TESS.

- **Simplified:** Real dips are often far smaller than 1%; the game makes them easier to see.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Exoplanets, ways to find a planet](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** light-delay

## Counting in primes

In 1974 the Arecibo telescope beamed a message to space. It had 1,679 bits, which is 23 × 73, two primes, so aliens could arrange it into a picture.

- **Simplified:** The Orrin glyphs are invented, inspired by this idea.
- **Source:** [SETI Institute: The Arecibo Message](https://www.seti.org/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** —

## Iron stops the furnace

Stars make energy by fusing light elements into heavier ones. That works all the way up to iron, but fusing iron uses up energy. In a massive star, the iron core collapses and the star explodes as a supernova.

- **Simplified:** Real stars don't get ingredients poured in! A star's mass mostly decides its fate.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Stars](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** star-change

## Big stars live fast

Massive blue stars burn out in a few million years. Small red dwarfs can shine for trillions of years, longer than the universe has existed so far.

- **Simplified:** The game uses the rule lifetime ≈ 10 billion years × mass^-2.5.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Stars](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** star-change

## Red giants

When a Sun-like star runs low on hydrogen in its core, it swells into a red giant, then sheds its outer layers and leaves a white dwarf.

- **Simplified:** The game's time-lapse skips the detailed stages.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Stars](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** star-change

## Our Sun's long future

Our Sun is about halfway through its life. In roughly 5 billion years it will become a red giant. Modern humans have been around for about 300,000 years.

- **Simplified:** Scientists also estimate Earth may get too warm for oceans in about a billion years, still an enormous span of time.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: The Sun](https://science.nasa.gov/sun/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** star-change

## Space weather

Solar flares are bursts of light and radiation. Coronal mass ejections (CMEs) are huge clouds of charged gas that can shake Earth's magnetic field and damage satellites.

- **Simplified:** In the game a CME gives the ship a gentle shove; a real one wouldn't noticeably push a heavy spacecraft.
- **Source:** [NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center](https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** —

## Planet-wide dust storms

In 2018 a dust storm covered all of Mars and blocked so much sunlight that the solar-powered Opportunity rover went silent.

- **Simplified:** Cinder's storms are shorter than real Martian ones.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Opportunity](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-exploration-rovers-spirit-and-opportunity/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** —

## Why the Moon has phases

Half of the Moon is always lit by the Sun. Phases come from how much of that lit half we see from Earth, not from Earth's shadow.

- **Simplified:** The game's moon-phase calculation can be off by a few hours.
- **Source:** [NASA Science: Moon Phases](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/) ⚠ verify
- **Concepts:** —
